This folder contains historical documents which support the claims made in the comment of GM2020. The documents are cited in the comment. 

## 1806_Magistrato_di_Revisione

The document supports the claim that copyrights were not enforced in Venice prior to 1806, not 1801 as GM2020 claims. This image is part of the case of Sonzogno and Graziosi. The image itself can be found in the Archivio di State a Milano. It is in the collection 'Atti di governo, Studi parte moderna'. It details the response of ministry to the request of Sonzogno in April 1806 to have the authorities halt Graziosi, in Venice, in republishing works Sonzogno had copyright under the 1801 Cisalpine law. The memo explains that the newly annexed territories, including Venice, did not have copyright laws enforced, as they were not part of the Cisalpine Republic when the law had passed.

## 1806_Raccolta_Regno_Italia

The document supports the claim that copyrights protections were expanded to territories brought under Napoleonic rule after 1801, contrary to what GM2020 claim.This is an extract of the bulleting of laws passed in the Kingdom of Italy (the successor to the Cisalpine Republic) in 1806. Decree 112, passed in 1806, explicitly expands copyright protections to the newly acquired territories, and Venice is specifically mentioned. 

## 1811_Bullettino_Regno_Napoli

This document further provides evidence that copyright introduction was not limited to Lombardy and Venetia, contrary to the claim made in GM2020. This document is a bulletin of laws published in the Kingdom of Naples in 1811. Decree 1134 lays out a system for the functioning of theatres and performances. It is inspired by French laws; the ruler of the Kingdom was Napoleon's brother-in-law Murat. It includes the introduction of copyrights and mentions that impressarios cannot stage repeat performances without renumerating composers. 

## Raccolta_1813_Po

This document further provides evidence that copyright introduction was not limited to Lombardy and Venetia, contrary to the claim made in GM2020. In particular, it shows that Italian territories that were annexed by France upheld French laws. This document is an extract of a message by General Lameth, French prefect to the department of Po (containing Turin and other Piemontese territories), to the mayors of the department. He repeats that the mayors have a duty to uphold the French law of 1791 regaring copyright.
